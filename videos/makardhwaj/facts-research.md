# makardhwaj: fact research

Researched 2026-11-03. Research only; script.md, shots and video files were not touched.

## (a) What to name aloud

- Birth of Makardhwaj from Hanuman's sweat: the **Ananda Ramayana** (medieval Sanskrit text, Sara Kanda; Wikipedia: "a later medieval text composed during the 15th century AD"). It is cited as Ananda Ramayana 1.9.216 on wisdomlib (Jaina essay quoting Bulcke). Other works named for the fish-drinks-sweat motif: Jaimini Bharata (Meravana carita), the Gujarati Narmakathakosa.
- The Patala gate fight and the crowning: told in **popular and regional retellings** of the Ahiravana / Mahiravana episode. I could not pin the gatekeeper and fight scenes to a single named old text. In the Thai *Ramakien* the equivalent figure is Macchanu (different mother).
- It is NOT in Valmiki Ramayana.
- Do NOT name Krittivasa as the source of Makardhwaj. The Krittivasa Mahiravana episode I opened has the Patala kidnapping but no Makardhwaj. Suggested line for the voice: "कथा आनंद रामायण और कई क्षेत्रीय रामकथाओं में मिलती है"; the existing s4 line ("रामायण की एक लोकप्रिय क्षेत्रीय परंपरा") is acceptable but vague.

## (d) Open doubts

- I could not reach the Ananda Ramayana text itself. The "1.9.216" reference is second-hand from wisdomlib (a Jaina critical study quoting Bulcke), and the wisdomlib definition page credits the Puranic Encyclopaedia. Chapter and verse should be checked in an edition before it is printed.
- That wisdomlib essay does NOT describe the Patala gatekeeper or the fight. The gate scene is therefore sourced only to popular and encyclopaedic secondary pages (Wikipedia, Bharat Discovery, Sanskriti Magazine, Sivananda). No scholarly or primary text for the gate fight was found.
- Bharat Discovery's page wrongly labels its account the "Valmiki Ramayana version". That contradicts every other source (and Wikipedia) that says the story is absent from Valmiki. Treat that page with caution.
- Whether the Krittivasa Ramayana contains Makardhwaj is not settled by me. The greenmesg page is a summary; the original Bengali was not read. Search results that named Krittivasa came from a single blog. Do not name it unless a better source appears.
- Names differ across texts: Krittivasa (per the summary) makes Mahiravana the king and Ahiravana his son; others use the two names for one king. The script's "Ahiravana, king of Patala" follows the common popular usage.
- The tail-in-the-sea detail is not in the Valmiki chapters I read (5.53, 5.54), but I did not read the whole Sundara Kanda. Do not assert it as Valmiki.
- The author's source tag "nkbashram.org" was not opened.
- Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool; re-check any quote before it goes in the description.
