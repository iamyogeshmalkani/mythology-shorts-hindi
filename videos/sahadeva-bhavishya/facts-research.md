# sahadeva-bhavishya: facts research

## (a) Name aloud
**लोककथा / क्षेत्रीय परंपरा** (popular and regional retellings). The curse that his head would burst is **not in Vyasa's Mahabharata**. I could not find the earliest source.
- Vyasa gives Sahadeva one claim of knowledge: "I know the present, the past, and the future" of cattle only (Virata Parva, Sec. X).
- The closest attested regional versions I found are the Odia **Sarala Mahabharata** (boon from Ashwini Kumara; he must answer when asked) and **Telugu folk additions** (Krishna threatens a head-splitting curse). Neither is a clean match to the script.

## (d) Open doubts
- **Earliest source of the head-bursting curse: not found.** Possibilities I could not check: Jaimini Bharata, Kashidasi, Pandava Gita, Kannada and Telugu regional epics. My searches returned no hit on these. Say "लोककथा", not a text name.
- **The Subba Rao paper** (*Asian Ethnology*, "Telugu Folk Additions to Maha Bharatha") could not be opened (404 on https://asianethnology.org/article/1649/download). My account of it, with the finger episode and the thousand-pieces threat, comes only from a search excerpt. Open the paper before quoting it.
- **Sarala and the Telugu folk story** are not Vyasa. They differ from each other, and neither says "head will burst if he tells anyone". Sarala is closest to "he knew the future" and "silence blamed"; Telugu is closest to "head splits".
- **Vyasa's Sahadeva.** I checked the Virata Parva statement and the Mahaprasthanika reason. I did not search the Sabha or Udyoga Parvas for his counsel, or for any astrology passage.
- **Reliability of sources.** Most pages that repeat the curse are popular sites (mahabharataonline.com, mythlok.com, blogs) with no named text. mythlok.com and meghnaunni.com were seen only in search results.
- **Absence in Vyasa.** I read only the Pandu-death section. This is not a proof of absence across the whole epic.
- **The ibiblio section numbers** are one lower than the page number in some books. I took the section number from each page's own heading.
