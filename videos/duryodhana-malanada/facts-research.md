# Fact research: duryodhana-malanada

Researched 2026-11-03. Nothing in script, shots or video files was changed.

## (a) Tradition to name aloud

This is a **local Kerala temple legend** (Kollam district), told by the temple itself and repeated by news and travel sites. It is NOT in the Mahabharata. Say it once, early: "केरल की स्थानीय मान्यता के अनुसार".

Correct name and place (Wikipedia, the temple's own site and Kerala Tourism agree on the main points):
- Poruvazhy **Peruviruthy Malanada** Temple (also written Poruvazhi / Peruvazhi / Peruvruthy), Edakkad ward, Poruvazhy village, **Kunnathoor taluk**, Kollam district. "Malanada" is the hill; the shrine is also called a "Mala Appooppan" shrine, and Duryodhana is affectionately "Appooppan / Malayappooppan".
- No idol and no sanctum. What is worshipped: a raised platform (althara / mandapam), where devotees focus through *sankalpam* (a mental act of devotion). The "Sankalpa Moorthy" is believed to be Duryodhana.
- Legend (temple's own version): while searching for the Pandavas during THEIR exile, Duryodhana reached Malanada hill, tired and thirsty, and asked at a house for water. An elderly woman gave him **toddy** as a sign of respect. He saw from her dress that she was Kurava (a community then treated as untouchable), accepted it, worshipped Shiva on the hill for the people's welfare, and gave farmland to the devaswom. Land tax is still levied in his name.
- Community: the temple site and Curly Tales say the priest is from the Kurava community; Wikipedia and keralatemples.info say the temple is run by a committee elected from seven *karas*, with NSS and SNDP union representatives and two family groups (one Kurava, one Ezhava) holding special status.
- Festival (not in script): Malakkuda Mahotsavam / Malanada Kettukazhcha, March (Malayalam month Meenam), flag-hoisting on the first Friday, giant Kala and Kuthira structures, Kathakali "Nizhalkuthu" at night.

## (d) Open doubts

1. **No scholarly source found.** Everything is the temple's own account, journalism, tourism boards and blogs. No Sahapedia, academic paper, or The Hindu / India Today article was opened (the author's "India Today" tag was not found). The legend should be told as the temple's tradition, never as history.
2. **The legend varies.** Who helped Duryodhana (old woman with toddy, old man with water and food, Kurava chief with food and shelter) differs by source. The toddy-by-an-elderly-Kurava-woman version is the temple's own and the most common; say only "एक बुज़ुर्ग महिला" or "स्थानीय लोग".
3. **A blog comment (2024, unsourced) disputes the origin**, claiming it is ancestor worship and that the Duryodhana story is recent. Not reliable, but it shows the origin story is contested; another reason to say "मान्यता है".
4. **Prasad claim** could not be confirmed; drop it.
5. **Current toddy practice.** The temple's own history page mentions toddy only inside the legend. News (Deccan Chronicle, 2019) and tourist sites say liquor is a main offering today. I have not seen a temple-run source saying how offerings are handled.
6. The "वनवास" error probably came from the draft's wording; the fix above keeps the user's sentence otherwise intact.
