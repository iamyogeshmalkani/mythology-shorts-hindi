# urmila-neend: fact research

Researched 2026-11-03. Research only; script.md, shots and video files were not touched.

## (a) What to name aloud

- Name this as a **folk / oral tradition, not Valmiki**: "तेलुगु महिलाओं के रामायण-गीतों की परंपरा" (Telugu women's Ramayana songs, the song usually titled *Urmiladevi Nidra*), as presented by Prof. V. Narayana Rao in *Manushi* issue 153 (2006). Devdutt Pattanaik also places it in "folk Ramayanas or Ram-kathas", especially South Indian ones.
- Do NOT name Valmiki, Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas), Kamba or Krittivasa as the source of the sleep story. I found nothing tying any of them to it.
- Earliest attestation: I could not find a dated earliest source. The oldest tradition I can name is the oral Telugu women's songs. English Wikipedia's Urmila and Lakshmana pages give only modern retellings (Kavita Kane, *Sita's Sister*, 2014; Tulika Singh, 2018) for the Nidra scene.
- The author's fact note ("यह कथा लोकपरंपरा में प्रचलित है, वाल्मीकि रामायण में इसका वर्णन नहीं है") is SUPPORTED. Keep it.

## (d) Open doubts

- All page text and quotes came through a summarising fetch tool, not a raw reader. Re-read the quoted lines on the live pages before printing them in the description.
- The Manushi PDF would not parse; I used the HTML version of the article. I did not see the full song text, so I cannot say whether Nidra Devi appears in that Telugu song. The Nidra scene is only confirmed in modern retellings.
- The author's source tag ("UC Press E-Books Collection Archive, 1982-2004") is unverified. The one UC Press title I met is B. A. van Nooten's *Ramayana* (2000), cited on Wikipedia only for the Indrajit 14-year boon, not for Urmila's sleep. I could not open it.
- The hindu-blog claim that the Telugu Ranganatha Ramayana (Buddha Reddy) has the Nidra scene rests on one blog. I could not confirm it. Do not name that text.
- I could not date the first appearance of the sleep story. The 1986 book *Streela Ramayanapu Patalu* (Telugu women's Ramayana songs) appeared only in a search snippet; I did not open it.
- Manushi's line that Valmiki mentions Urmila "only once, in two lines" is loose: she is named in Valmiki 1.71, 1.72 and 1.73.
- I checked Valmiki only in the passages listed. "Not in Valmiki" is therefore "not found in the passages I read, plus Pattanaik and Narayana Rao say so", not a full-text search.
