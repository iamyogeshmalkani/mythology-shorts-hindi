# barbarik-khatu-shyam: facts research

## (a) Name aloud
- Barbarika himself: **Skanda Purana, Maheshvara Khanda, Kaumarika Khanda, chs. 59-66** (the Guptakshetra / Mahisagara-sangama section; Barbarika's birth is ch. 60, his death ch. 66). Not in Vyasa's Mahabharata.
- The "Brahmin disguise, asks for the head as charity, promise to the mother to side with the losing party, name Shyam" parts are the **Khatu Shyam / Rajasthani popular tradition**. They are **not in the Skanda Purana text I read (ch. 66)**, which tells it differently (see rows 7-9).
- Say aloud: "खाटू श्याम की लोककथा के अनुसार", and "स्कंद पुराण में बर्बरीक की कथा थोड़ी अलग है".

## (d) Open doubts
- **Skanda chapters 59 and 61-65 not read.** The three arrows, the boons from the goddesses, and the mother's advice may sit in ch. 61-65. I read only ch. 60 (birth) and ch. 66 (death). Wisdomlib's Sanskrit page was 403. A reader of Tagare's whole Kaumarika Khanda should check ch. 61-65 before the "three arrows" and "promise" lines are called folk-only. In ch. 60 the arrows, the Siddha-ambika boon and the promise are not mentioned. Hiltebeitel (p. 431) is cited by Wikipedia for the arrows from Durga. I could not open his book.
- **The Brahmin disguise, the dan, and the mother's promise.** I found them in Wikipedia and its mirror, and in devotional pages, but no scholarly or older text. They are attested for "the Khatu Shyam tradition", with no date.
- **Name "Shyam".** Not in Skanda ch. 66 as read. Origin of the title, and "Suhridaya" (an essay says Krishna named him that), unverified.
- **Temple history.** Not in the script, but sources seen are devotional or news (a legend of the head found at Shyam Kund; a date 1027 on a devotional site). Do not add.
- **The author's tag** mahabharataonline.com: popular site; only its search-result title was seen, and the page was never opened.
- **Tool caveat.** Quotes from wisdomlib (Tagare) came through a summarising fetch tool. Re-read ch. 66 before quoting it in the description.
- **Second reading of ch. 66.** The summary said the chapter does not name the discus Sudarshana and does not call him Shyam. Treat the "no Brahmin / no request" reading as one summariser's reading until a human has confirmed it.
